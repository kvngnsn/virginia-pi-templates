## Requirements and primary authorities

Current [Virginia Rules of Court](https://www.vacourts.gov/static/courts/scv/rulesofcourt.pdf) were downloaded and compared with the existing snapshot; SHA-256 is `15a5095aba5043b863d2c7fb26f24c2556cad4908054874c0dff4f770af53a0f`. The [amendments index](https://www.vacourts.gov/courts/scv/amend) was checked for effective changes.

- Rule 1:12 governs later papers and requires the actual date and method of service; emailing requires Rule 1:17 authority or signed written consent. Its additional mail/fax certificate requirement is expressly subject to Rule 1:17.
- Rules 1:5 and 1:17 address counsel, unrepresented parties and authorized electronic practice. A certificate belongs at the foot of the served document; this reusable block may be appended rather than separately docketed.
- The collected Court of Appeals certificate is **appellate-specific** under Rule 5A:1(c). It was not adopted as circuit-court authority or an initial-process return.

## Conditional decisions

- Use only an actual completed service event, all required recipients, and the authorized method for each recipient. A request to draft does not establish service.
- Do not assume email service from the existence of an email address or silently convert failed delivery into valid service.
- Original circuit-court text is intentionally distinct from the previously downloaded appellate sample.

## Presentation choices

The parenthesis caption, Times New Roman 12 and one-inch margins follow the existing circuit-court house style. Double spacing follows the court-paper baseline; no copied publisher example facts or completed signature is included.

## Required case inputs

Document and attachments, actual service date/method, counsel and unrepresented-party identities, addresses, electronic-service basis and any required certificate transmittal.

## Verification and limits

Original clean drafting skeleton, with required sections and unresolved facts visible. Review of the blank template does not establish case-filled legal accuracy. Signatures, completed service, fees, enclosures and judicial acts must never be inferred from the request to draft.
